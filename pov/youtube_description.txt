You're standing on a manhole cover in the middle of Times Square at 7:12 PM when it starts to sink, and it doesn't stop. You drop past the sewer, the steam pipes and a passing subway train, into bedrock more than a billion years old, deeper than any cave, mine or borehole. The rock starts to glow. You fall through the mantle, past diamonds and blue crystals that hold water, into an ocean of liquid iron, and float weightless at the centre of the Earth. Then "down" turns into "up", and you rise toward the one place on the planet exactly opposite Times Square, out through the seafloor, past a sperm whale, and into the sunrise on the other side of the world.

What is real: every depth on screen, the order of the layers and the facts in the captions. Times Square to its exact opposite point is 12,742 km straight through the centre of the Earth, and that point is in the southern Indian Ocean at 40.76° S, 106.01° E, about 1,070 km from Australia. New York's deepest subway station is 55 m down, and City Water Tunnel No. 3, begun in 1970, runs about 180 m under the city. The deepest known cave reaches 2,212 m, the deepest mines about 4 km, and the deepest hole ever drilled, the Kola Superdeep Borehole, stopped at 12,262 m in 1989, where the rock was 180 °C. The mantle makes up 84% of Earth's volume, and diamonds form below about 150 km. The core begins at 2,890 km: liquid iron and nickel, bigger than the planet Mars, and its currents create Earth's magnetic field, which last flipped about 780,000 years ago. The inner core is solid iron about as hot as the surface of the Sun, kept solid by about 3.6 million atmospheres. At the centre of the Earth gravity really does cancel out, and you would be weightless. On the far side, the ocean floor is only about 7 km thick and is built from the same layers you pass: gabbro, sheeted dikes, pillow lava and mud made of tiny shells. In the deep sea about three in four animals make their own light, sperm whales hunt squid far below 1,000 m and can hold their breath for over an hour, and the last trace of sunlight fades at about 1,000 m.

What is not real, or not known: nobody could survive this, and no tunnel like this could stay open, because the rock is far too hot and too squeezed. The speed is chosen so the trip takes 15 minutes. In a real fall you would be fastest at the centre, not slowest; here you slow down so you can see it. The small cavity at the centre is invented. The geode, the pocket of peridot, the kimberlite pipe and the sinking slab of ancient ocean floor are real kinds of things, placed where you can see them. The layers are textbook averages: under New York the depth of the mantle (about 36 km), the rock temperatures and the thickness of each deep layer are estimates. Nobody has ever seen deeper than about 12 km, so everything below that is known from earthquake waves and laboratory experiments. That the transition zone holds as much water as all the oceans is a hypothesis, and how the crystals of the inner core line up is still debated. The depth of the seafloor at the exit point is an estimate. Animals are drawn larger and closer than you would meet them, and in the dark they are lit by your wrist light.

You crossed the planet in fifteen minutes. In a real hole with no air to slow you down, the fall would take about 38 minutes, and you would reach the other side at exactly zero speed. If nobody caught you there, you would fall all the way back to Times Square.
