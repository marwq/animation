🛡 THE ADVICE IN THIS FILE IS REAL
• Do not go downstairs. Do not confront an intruder. Things can be replaced. You cannot.
• Lock yourself in a room. Wedge a chair under the handle, push heavy furniture against the door.
• Bring everyone in the house into the same room. Sit away from the door and the window.
• Call your emergency number. Give your address first.
• Can't speak in the UK? Dial 999, then press 55 when asked (the Silent Solution).
• In many parts of the US you can text 911. Call if you can, text if you can't.
• Turn your screen brightness down. Stay on the line.
• Have an alarm with a panic button? Press it. No alarm? Press the panic button on your car keys.
• Do not open the door to a voice. Ask the dispatcher to confirm the officers are really at your door.

📁 THE CASES ARE REAL TOO
• U.S. Bureau of Justice Statistics (2003–2007): someone was home in about 28% of burglaries; in 7% of all burglaries a household member was attacked or threatened
• "Hot prowl": the police term for a burglary while the occupants are home
• 2008, Kasuya (Fukuoka), Japan: a man noticed food disappearing, set up cameras, and police found a woman who had been living on the top shelf of his closet for about a year
• "Phrogging": secretly living in someone else's home while they still live in it
• 2012, UNC Charlotte: about 60% of 422 incarcerated burglars said an alarm would send them to another house
• Red House, West Virginia: a homeowner found a burglar asleep in his bed; he was still asleep when deputies arrived
