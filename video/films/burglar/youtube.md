```
There are seven rules. Six of them come from the police. The seventh comes from the burglar.

FILE 018 // OFFICE OF DOMESTIC CONTINUITY

The advice in this file is real:
• Do not go downstairs. Do not confront an intruder.
• Lock yourself in a room. Wedge a chair, push furniture against the door.
• Call for help. If you cannot speak in the UK: dial 999, then press 55 (Silent Solution).
  In much of the US you can text 911 — call if you can, text if you can't.
• Stay on the line. Let the dispatcher confirm when officers are at your door.
• If you have an alarm or a car key with a panic button, use it.

The cases are real too:
• U.S. Bureau of Justice Statistics (2003–2007): someone was home in about 28% of burglaries;
  in 7% of all burglaries a household member was attacked or threatened
• "Hot prowl": police term for a burglary while the occupants are home
• 2008, Kasuya (Fukuoka), Japan: a woman lived on the top shelf of a man's closet for about a year
• "Phrogging": secretly living in someone else's home
• 2012, UNC Charlotte: about 60% of 422 incarcerated burglars said an alarm would send them elsewhere
• Red House, West Virginia: a homeowner found a burglar asleep in his bed; he was still asleep when deputies arrived
```
